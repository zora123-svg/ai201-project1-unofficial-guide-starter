Understanding the St. Paul Crime Rate - How Crime Rates Are Calculated

How safe is St. Paul MN? Before answering this question, it is important to understand how crime rates work to begin with. Crime rates are determined by data from local law enforcement agencies and are then officially reported by the FBI. When determining the overall crime rate, the FBI takes a look at the total number of crimes committed in cities per 1,000 residents and then divides it by the area's population. This number is then multiplied by 100, which is why crime rates are reported as, "X crimes per 100,000 people."

Crime rates are often divided into three categories: Overall crime rate, violent crime rate, and property crime rate. Then, you can get even more specific and look at categories such as homicide, motor vehicle theft, and burglary.

While the crime rate in St. Paul MN is useful when looking at information for the city as a whole, it is not reliable as a primary source. The St. Paul crime rate is city-wide, meaning that it does not differentiate between areas and neighborhoods in the city. Oftentimes, different neighborhoods, or even streets, have major differences in crime levels. This is especially true in a large city like St. Paul.

Additionally, reporting crime information is completely voluntary, making some cities appear to have a higher crime rate, when, in reality, they simply did not report some or all of their crime statistics.
